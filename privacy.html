Privacy Policy

Last updated 1 December 2026.

This policy explains what personal information Verity collects, why we use it, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and the rights you have.

We have written it in plain English so you can read it once and know where you stand. Short legal names (POPIA, GDPR) appear where they help a regulator. They do not change the plain meaning.

Verity is a midlife wellness app for women aged 18 and over. The app is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or give medication doses.

This policy covers:

  • the Verity app for iPhone and iPad (bundle id com.veritywomen.app), shown as Verity Woman in the App Store, and for Android (package app.verity.mobile) in Google Play; and
  • the website https://veritywomen.com.

2. What we collect

Most of what Verity holds is health information. The law treats that as especially sensitive: "special personal information" under POPIA, and "special-category data" under the GDPR. We only collect it if you choose to use Verity and give the consents described in section 3.

You can use Verity without an account. Then your record stays on that phone. An account is optional. It is how we keep a copy of some of your record if the phone is lost, and it is required to invite a support person or to ask live Ask Vera in your own words.

Account (only if you create one)

  • Email address, as Apple or Google gives it to us when you sign in.
  • New accounts have no password. Accounts made before October 2026 could use one. For those we store a one-way hash, not the password itself. We cannot read it back.
  • If you sign in with Google on Android, Google confirms who you are. We receive a token, your email, a Google user id, and, the first time, your name. We do not receive your Google contacts, Drive, photos, or search history.
  • If you sign in with Apple on iPhone or iPad, Apple confirms who you are. We receive an identity token. We receive your email only if you choose to share it (Apple may give us a Hide My Email relay address). The first time you authorise Verity, Apple may also send a name. We do not receive your Apple contacts, iCloud files, photos, or payment details.
  • Each phone shows one way in: Continue with Apple on iPhone and iPad, Continue with Google on Android.

Profile

What you tell us about yourself in the app, for example: display name; age or date of birth; life stage (for example, regular cycles, perimenopause, menopause, postmenopause); whether you are on HRT; the concerns you pick during set-up.

An optional profile photo stays on that phone. We store a file name in your profile so the app can show it. We do not copy the photo into our cloud backup.

Health and cycle logs

What you choose to log, for example:

  • cycle entries (period days, flow, spotting);
  • symptoms, including mood, mental-health, sexual and intimate-health symptoms, and how strong they felt;
  • energy and sleep;
  • food and exercise;
  • free-text notes you type;
  • symptoms you name yourself, in your own words. These are never sent to live Ask Vera.

HRT

Hormone-therapy type, how you take it, dose, brand, start date, changes, and how you said you felt.

Health-background survey

Longer "health background" answers (the in-app health-profile survey) stay on that phone. They are not copied into your cloud account.

Documents

Letters, scans and similar files you add in Verity stay on that phone. There is no documents upload to our servers.

Doctor report

You can build a PDF to take to a clinician. That file is created on your phone. If you share it (email, messaging, files), it leaves through the app you choose, under that app's rules. We do not receive a copy of the share. Verity deletes its own copy from your phone 30 minutes after the share menu closes. If you close Verity before then, the copy is deleted the next time you open it. A copy you sent or saved somewhere else stays there.

Payments

Verity takes no payments yet; this section is added when paid launches.

Tell us what you think

When you send us a note from the app, we get your words, the topic you tapped, the app version, the kind of phone and its system version, and your email only if you typed one. Nothing else: not your logs, notes, symptoms, name or account. The note is emailed to support@veritywomen.com, is not stored on our server, and the email is deleted after 12 months. You can send one without an account.

Support-person mode

If you invite someone, we store: a one-time invite code (it lasts about three days); the link between your accounts; the categories you have turned on for them, if any; and notes they write about how you seemed.

They see nothing of yours until you opt in, category by category. The categories you can share are: symptoms, sleep and energy, cycle, and HRT. Vera chats, the health-background survey, and documents cannot be shared. There is no switch for those.

If you are the support person, we store the notes you write about the person you support, and your own account details. You do not get their health record unless they turn a category on.

Vera: ready answers (Ask Vera)

Ask Vera's ready answers are pre-written articles from a cited evidence library, served on your device. Reading them does not send the question to an AI model and does not need a network call for the answer. We do not collect the text of those taps as a health record.

Those articles are general educational information from cited sources. A clinician hasn't reviewed them yet. They are not medical advice.

Vera: live Ask Vera (optional, uses an AI model)

You type a question in your own words.

  • The app sends the question to the Verity API on our server in Cape Town, South Africa, which sends it to Anthropic (the company that makes the Claude AI model), in the United States, so the model can draft an answer.
  • Anthropic processes your question for us under its commercial terms, which bar it from training its models on our data, and its data-processing terms. Section 4 says how long it keeps what we send.
  • Ask Vera is not medical advice and is not a medical device.
  • We do not claim HIPAA, a Business Associate Agreement, or that health data never leaves the phone.
  • Sent with the question, so the answer can fit you: the last few messages of the same conversation (so a follow-up makes sense), your age, life stage, whether you are on HRT and for how many days, your most common recent symptoms, and how you have been eating. Not your name or email, and never the symptoms you named yourself. What you type in your question is sent as you wrote it.
  • Extra consent ticks appear before your first live question. You must agree that Vera is AI, not a doctor, and that your question, with the context listed above, goes to Anthropic in the United States, where privacy law may protect it less than South Africa's. None of them is ticked for you. If your earlier live Ask Vera tick didn't say where your questions go, you are asked again before your next live question.
  • You need an account. There is a monthly allowance: 15 new live questions a calendar month. After that, you can still get up to 5 more replies in a chat you already have open.
  • Crisis, dose, or otherwise unsafe questions are meant to be stopped on our server. A stopped question doesn't reach the model, isn't stored as a conversation, and doesn't use up a question. No check is perfect.
  • If the live call fails, you see an honest "unavailable" message. The app does not invent a stand-in health answer.

What we log on the server about live use: a user id and an outcome code (for example, that an answer was given, or that a question was blocked). Never the question text, the answer text, or the API key, in those logs.

Where the conversation itself lives:

  • Without an account, any on-device demo chat stays on that phone.
  • The one-time cloud backup when you first sign in copies logs, HRT and profile. It does not copy documents, the health-background survey, or Vera chats that were already on the phone.
  • If you use live Ask Vera while signed in, we keep that conversation with your account on our server in Cape Town, South Africa, so it can show again in the app. Deleting your account deletes it.

Live answers are AI-generated and can be wrong. A clinician hasn't reviewed them.

If you report an answer (the Report link under it), we store which answer it was, the reason you tapped, and when. We do not copy the words into the report. Someone at Verity then reads that answer and the question you asked, so we can fix what went wrong. Your report is deleted when you delete your account.

Vera who remembers you (only with your separate yes)

Vera can use your own logs to answer you. That means more of your health information goes to Anthropic than for a plain live question. So it has its own consent screen, and nothing from your logs goes to the AI until you say yes there. Its box is never ticked for you.

  • What is sent: a summary of your last 90 days of logs (your symptoms and how strong they felt, your cycle, sleep and energy, your HRT and its changes, and food and exercise), together with your question and the context listed under live Ask Vera. Not your name or email, and not the symptoms you named yourself, your free-text notes, your documents or your health-background survey.
  • Who receives it, and where: Anthropic, in the United States, under the same terms as live Ask Vera (section 4). Privacy law in the United States may protect your information less than South Africa's. The consent screen says this before you choose.
  • Changing your mind: you can turn it off any time in Settings → Privacy & data. From then on, no log summary is sent. What was already sent is handled as section 4 describes.

If you say no, Vera still answers your questions and the rest of the app keeps working.

Consent records

Which boxes you ticked, the consent version shown, and the time. None of the boxes is pre-ticked. The four onboarding consents are:

  1. storing your health data on your device and in the cloud;
  2. that Vera is an AI companion, not a medical professional;
  3. that you have read and agree to the Terms of Use and this Privacy Policy, and understand that section 11 of the Terms limits what Verity can be held responsible for;
  4. that you are 18 or older.

Live Ask Vera has its own extra ticks, stored with a separate version stamp, before the first live question. Vera who remembers you has its own as well.

If you join as a support person, you tick the same four kinds of box. The health-data box is about notes you write about the person you support, not about your own hormones.

Technical data

To answer the app, our server necessarily sees ordinary connection information when a request arrives, including the internet (IP) address it came from. Our server logs record that a signed-in request reached the server, roughly when, and which account the sign-in belongs to. They do not keep IP addresses, and they are deleted after 14 days. We do not collect advertising identifiers. We do not embed advertising or social-media tracking SDKs. We do not currently use a separate crash-reporting company (for example Sentry or Crashlytics).

If you turn on Help improve Verity (Settings → Privacy & data; it starts off), we record how the app is used, not what you log. For example: that the app opened, which screens you visited, that a log was saved, which ready answer or video you opened and how far you watched (the name of an answer or video can show the topic you were reading about, for example hot flushes), and whether a live Ask Vera question was answered. Each event carries two random numbers, one for this installation of the app and one for each time you use it, plus the app version and the phone's system version. It is never linked to your account, your name or your email. It never includes your health logs, HRT details, the symptoms you logged, notes, chat, or anything you typed. A copy is kept on our server in Cape Town, South Africa, and deleted after 90 days.

While Help improve Verity is on, we also send the event and screen names to Google Analytics (Firebase), on iPhone and Android. We add no other details. Google's own software adds some technical data of its own: a random app number, the phone model and system version, and a rough location worked out from the internet address. Nothing is sent to Google until you turn Help improve Verity on. Developer copies of the app are not sent to Google. If you say no, or turn the switch off, nothing more is sent from then on, and any events still waiting on the phone are thrown away.

On the website (veritywomen.com), we use Google Analytics to understand which pages people open and how the site is used. Google receives ordinary visit data (for example IP address used to estimate location, device and browser type, and pages viewed). That is technical visit data for the marketing site. It is not your in-app health record.

Reminders

If you turn reminders on (morning or evening check-in, a weekly period check-in, or an HRT check-in a few weeks after a change), they are scheduled on your phone. We do not currently send those reminders through a separate push server. The app uses Expo's notifications library to talk to your phone's own reminder system.

Website

If you visit veritywomen.com or join a waitlist, we keep the email address you type so we can write to you about Verity, and we send a confirmation email. You agree to this when you join. Every email has a way to unsubscribe, and you can ask us to delete that address.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell your data.
  • We do not share it with advertisers or ad networks.
  • We do not use your health data to target ads.
  • We do not receive or store your card or bank details.
  • We do not put a provider API key or model id in the app.
  • We do not claim HIPAA or a Business Associate Agreement.

5. How long we keep data

  • Account, profile, logs, HRT, support-person link and notes, live Vera conversations we store for a signed-in account, any log summaries we keep for Vera who remembers you, reports of a Vera answer, and the live-use ledger (user id and outcome only): kept while the account is active. Deleted when you delete the account (section 7).
  • What Anthropic keeps of what we sent it: see section 4.
  • Consent records: kept with the account so we can show what you agreed to. They are deleted with the account today.
  • Health-background survey, documents, and profile photo: on the phone, until you delete them in the app, delete the account (which also clears the on-device store), or uninstall. Uninstalling does not by itself delete a cloud account.
  • Local-only use (no account): on that phone only, until you delete it in the app or uninstall.
  • Invite codes: about three days, or until used or cancelled.
  • Waitlist email: until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete it.
  • Notes from Tell us what you think, and emails you send us (including a request to delete your account): 12 months, so we can help you and show we did what you asked.
  • Server logs (for example that a request reached the server): 14 days, then deleted. They do not keep IP addresses.
  • Technical app-usage events (Help improve Verity): the copy on our server is deleted after 90 days. Because it is not linked to your account, we can't pick out your events, so deleting your account doesn't remove them; they expire on the same 90-day schedule. The copy sent to Google Analytics is kept by Google for the retention period set on that property (we keep it as short as the product allows). Turning the switch off stops new events. It does not erase what Google already received.
  • Database backups: we do not keep backups of the database at the moment, so when we delete something from it, it is gone. If we start keeping backups, we will update this policy to say how long they last.

We complete an account deletion well within 30 days, and usually at once.

Phone backups (iCloud, Google backup, and the like) are outside Verity. They may keep a copy under that provider's rules until that backup ages out.

6. Your rights

You have rights over your information. How they are named depends on where you live. In practice you can:

  • Access what we hold (POPIA s 23; GDPR Art. 15).
  • Correct what is wrong (POPIA s 24; GDPR Art. 16). Most fields in Verity can be edited in the app.
  • Delete (section 7; GDPR Art. 17; POPIA s 24).
  • Get a copy. Verity has a JSON export in Settings → Privacy & data (GDPR Art. 20 portability).
  • Object to or restrict certain uses (GDPR Arts. 18 and 21), and withdraw consent.
  • Complain to a regulator.

South Africa: Information Regulator. Website: https://inforegulator.org.za. POPIA complaints: POPIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za. Enquiries: enquiries@inforegulator.org.za. Woodmead North Office Park, 54 Maxwell Drive, Woodmead, Johannesburg, 2191. Toll-free: 0800 017 160.

EU and UK. The GDPR and UK GDPR apply to us where the law says they do, for example because we offer the app to people in the EU and UK. You can complain to your local data-protection authority (in the UK, the ICO). We do not have an office in the EU or UK.

United States. We do not sell personal information or health data, and we do not share it for advertising. Other US state laws differ (for example California's). We do not claim a full US-state privacy programme. If you have a request, email privacy@veritywomen.com and we will help.

You do not have to pay to use these rights. We may need to confirm it is you. We will answer within one month, and tell you if we need longer and why.

7. Deleting your account and data

In the app: Settings → Privacy & data. Type DELETE to confirm.

If you no longer have the app: see https://veritywomen.com/delete-account, or email privacy@veritywomen.com from the email address your account uses, with the subject "Delete my account". We'll confirm it is you before we delete anything. If you signed in with Apple's Hide My Email, or can't use that email address any more, tell us and we'll find another way to check it's you.

Deleting removes your data from our systems for good. It is not a "deactivate" that we can undo. It removes your sign-in record, your record on our server, and the Verity store on that phone. Some copies are outside our systems, not linked to you, or kept for a set time; section 5 and the delete-account page list them (what Anthropic already received, usage events not linked to your account, emails you sent us, and phone backups).

If you signed in with Apple, deleting your account also asks Apple to end Verity's access to your Apple sign-in. If Apple's system doesn't answer, we still delete your Verity account.

We complete this well within 30 days, and usually immediately.

We do not currently keep a "deletion receipt" beyond the email you sent us, if you asked by email.

Support-person notes

  • If you end the link, notes already written about you stay in your record. You can delete a note. You cannot edit someone else's note.
  • If you delete your account, notes about you go with your record.
  • If a support person deletes their account, the notes they wrote about you are removed from your record too. The app tells them that before they confirm.

Signing out is not deletion. Your cloud copy stays until you delete the account.

8. Data on your device

Verity keeps a copy of your record on the phone so the app works offline and feels fast.

That copy sits in Verity's own storage area. Your phone's operating system keeps other apps out of it. We do not add a separate layer of encryption on top of that store today.

What an account actually backs up: logs, HRT record, and profile. Documents, the health-background survey, and Vera chats already on the phone are not part of that backup. Live Ask Vera conversations you have while signed in are stored with the account as described in section 2.

Deleting the app removes local data. A device backup may still hold a copy. Use the in-app delete if you also need the server copy gone.

If you would rather nothing left the phone at all, use Verity without an account. Then we do not hold a cloud copy. You cannot invite a support person or use live Ask Vera that way.

9. Children

Verity is for adults aged 18 and older. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. The onboarding gate asks you to confirm you are 18 or older. If we learn we have data about a child, we delete it.

10. Security

What is true, stated plainly:

  • Traffic between the app and our API travels over HTTPS, so it is encrypted on the way.
  • Cloud data requires sign-in.
  • The API checks every request against your account, so one account is not served another person's record.
  • The database is a managed MongoDB Atlas service. It needs its own login and is not public.
  • MongoDB Atlas keeps its storage disks encrypted as standard. We do not add our own extra encryption on top.
  • We do not add extra encryption on the phone store, beyond the phone's own app sandbox.

Once data is on the server, people who look after our server and database can reach what is on it. We do not claim the record is "encrypted and stored privately."

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects you, we will tell you and the relevant regulator, as the law requires. That includes POPIA's notification duties, the 72-hour regulator notice where the GDPR applies, and, in the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's Health Breach Notification Rule.
